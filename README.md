# Greendlinger lemma and Dehn's algorithm in Lean

This project targets the classical theorem that finite symmetrized group presentations satisfying (C'(1/6)) admit an effective Dehn word-problem solver.

The intended theorem surface combines:

1. **Greendlinger's lemma:** a nonempty freely reduced null word has a cyclic rotation containing a subword longer than half of a relator;
2. **Cyclic Dehn correctness:** replacing that subword by the inverse complementary arc strictly shortens the representative, preserves identity in the presented group up to conjugacy, terminates, and returns the empty word exactly for the identity element.

The implementation uses finite relator lists and Mathlib's reduced free-group words, so the candidate search and Dehn procedure are executable.

## Current formalization status

- The exhaustive redex search is proved complete for the stated factorization predicate.
- A finite executable (C'(1/6)) checker is proved equivalent to the piece definition, including repeated cyclic positions within a relator.
- Presented-group nullity has finite relator-certificate trees that flatten to products of conjugate relators, with a minimum-area certificate theorem.
- A minimum-area boundary seed is proved to contain no adjacent inverse relator-conjugate factors: deleting such a pair gives a strictly shorter certificate for the same word. This is an algebraic reducedness statement and is not yet a dipole-removal theorem for planar diagrams.
- `SmallCancellation/PlanarCancellation.lean` turns nested free-reduction trees into absolute source positions for cancelled pairs and retained letters. It also composes two reduction stages: the factorwise reductions of literal conjugate-relator boundaries, followed by the global reduction of the concatenated factor words. Later pairs and survivors are lifted through the earlier order-preserving source map. Lean proves that the combined trace is noncrossing, uses only inverse source letters, has distinct endpoints, keeps survivors ordered, excludes survivors from cancellation intervals, and partitions every source position exactly once. From these invariants, every position strictly inside a cancellation interval is shown to be an endpoint of a smaller pair; the first interior position starts a strictly nested pair. `IndexedBoundaryTrace` packages the core data. The module does not yet identify edges/vertices or build the relator faces into a planar map.
- `SmallCancellation/PlanarBoundarySeed.lean` applies the staged trace to a minimum-area relator certificate. `stagedBoundaryTrace` preserves the local-then-global cancellation pairing on the literal conjugate-relator boundary and carries the requested reduced boundary word as its ordered survivors. The area-one base case is discharged directly: any nontrivial null word with a certificate of area at most one contains its defining relator as a cyclic Dehn redex. This is still not a planar van Kampen diagram or a proof of the multi-face Greendlinger case.
- `SmallCancellation/ReducedLollipop.lean` now proves a conjugator-shortening normal form. For every factor conjugate to a relator in a finite symmetrized presentation, choose a conjugator of minimum reduced-word length. A cancellation at either lollipop junction would permit a cyclic rotation of the relator and a strictly shorter conjugator, contradicting minimality. Thus the literal `g r g⁻¹` boundary is reduced and equals the canonical factor word. The minimum-area seed packages these as reduced relator balloons; its balloon boundaries concatenate exactly to the certificate factor word, so `reducedLollipopBoundaryTrace` retains only the global noncrossing cancellations. Lean also verifies each balloon's exact length, `2 * stem + relator perimeter`, and the total boundary length, `|w| + 2 * cancellation count`. The trace is still not a planar diagram.
- A proof that a word is quotient-null now yields an ordered finite list of relator-conjugate factor occurrences with explicit conjugator and relator labels. The seed can retain a minimum-area relator certificate, and its occurrence count is proved equal to that area. Their literal `g r g⁻¹` boundary words freely reduce, factor by factor and then globally, to the requested reduced boundary word. The nested cancellation trees replay as adjacent inverse-letter deletions that preserve the free-group element, and each tree's pair count exactly accounts for its length difference. Minimum area rules out linear and cyclically adjacent inverse factor pairs, nonempty identity-product intervals, and nonempty cyclic identity-product intervals. This is proof-side algebraic boundary data, not yet a planar disk diagram.
- Kernel-checked examples accept a symmetrized relator with distinct cyclic letters, reject the periodic relator \(a^6\), run the cyclic word-problem procedure on an accepted C'(1/6) presentation, and exercise the reducer on \(\langle a\mid a^6\rangle\).
- Replacement is proved to preserve the presented-group element and strictly decrease free-word length.
- The fuel-bounded reducer is proved to terminate at an irreducible word.
- A cyclic redex search checks every rotation of the reduced word, including shells that cross the chosen boundary start.
- The cyclic fuel-bounded reducer preserves identity through each conjugating rotation. Its length invariant proves that valid fuel never increases the reduced length and that any available cyclic redex forces a strict decrease after the full run.
- Both linear and cyclic Dehn correctness are proved conditionally from their corresponding Greendlinger properties.
- Each curvature face stores actual corner angles with local 1/2 and 2/3 bounds; the face angle cap is derived by summation, and side/corner counts come from the same finite corner list. A finite Gauss--Bonnet accounting theorem derives total face curvature 2 from Euler's disk equation and aggregate vertex-angle/edge-side counts; the angle-counting lemma then forces a face with at most three internal arcs under its local bounds.
- `SmallCancellation/DiskMap.lean` adds a finite corner/edge incidence layer. It assigns corner angles from local vertex degree, proves the vertex angle total and edge-side total by finite-sum regrouping, and constructs the existing Gauss--Bonnet accounting record from those sums and Euler's equation. The record still assumes the local degree, edge-incidence, boundary-count, Euler, and face-classification properties; it does not yet encode cyclic orders or construct a planar map from a nullity certificate.
- A finite arithmetic lemma proves that three or fewer internal arcs, each strictly shorter than one sixth of the relator perimeter, leave an exterior shell arc longer than half the relator.
- Given a shell decomposition whose internal arcs are actual `C'(1/6)` pieces, the shell theorem constructs the cyclic redex consumed by the executable reducer.
- For symmetrized presentations, one-step relator rotation closure is proved to cover every cyclic cut, so shell arcs at arbitrary face positions yield the required same-perimeter prefix bounds.
- The same piece bounds show that a complete relator boundary needs at least seven arcs; with the model's angle cap, this derives nonpositive curvature for an internal face.
- Combining that result with finite Gauss--Bonnet accounting lets the curvature argument force a small shell when every internal face has explicit C'(1/6) piece data.
- The composed curvature-and-shell theorem returns the exact cyclic redex searched by the algorithm from that finite profile. A new incidence-based theorem derives the curvature accounting record from finite corner/edge data before invoking the shell argument. `CurvatureShellProfileProperty` now asks only for cyclically reduced boundary words; Mathlib's cyclic reduction theorem transfers the resulting shell occurrence across the removed conjugating stem. Profile existence for those boundaries remains the global diagram interface, and end-to-end Dehn correctness is conditional on it.
- `SmallCancellation/VertexLinks.lean` formalizes angle and Euler-defect accounting for circle, interval, and disconnected vertex links. It derives the generalized curvature ledger from finite link/corner incidence data, so cut and semi-exterior vertices can be represented without assuming a single simple boundary cycle. The incidence record still assumes its link shapes, corner incidences, edge-dart count, and Euler equation; it is not yet constructed from the minimum-area certificate or shown to be realized by a planar map.
- `SmallCancellation/FiniteDartEdges.lean` quotients a finite dart set by edge reversal, proves that every unoriented edge has exactly two orientations, and derives that the sum of out-degrees on finite incident-vertex support is twice the number of unoriented edges. Every retained vertex has positive degree. This supplies the finite dart-count identity used by link-based curvature data; it does not construct a planar rotation system or vertex links.
- `SmallCancellation/EdgeFold.lean` defines the quotient for one fold of oppositely traversed boundary darts. Lean proves that the edge-reversal involution descends to the quotient and that the selected dart pair and its twins are identified consistently. This is the local algebraic fold only: the quotient is not yet proved to remain a planar map or to supply the vertex-link and Euler data needed above.
- `SmallCancellation/DartGraphFold.lean` extends one quotient to source/target vertices and signed relator labels. It defines label-preserving graph maps and composes them, proves that a single adjacent free cancellation is realized as an edge fold, and replays a whole free-reduction certificate as successive folds while carrying the original graph into each quotient. Surjectivity of every fold map gives finite dart support for the quotient. `SmallCancellation/WordPath.lean` builds the occurrence path and polygonal boundary loop of every finite word, together with prefix and suffix embeddings and a prefix walk to the source of each edge occurrence. `SmallCancellation/LollipopFolds.lean` constructs every balloon's stem-pair list in the flattened seed boundary, joins each balloon's outer endpoint positions, and proves that every relator segment—including an empty-conjugator segment—is a closed labeled walk after stem and global cancellation folds. It also proves that each complete conjugate-relator boundary is a closed walk in the final quotient, alongside the relator subloop. `SmallCancellation/FoldedBoundarySeed.lean` restricts the quotient to the finite set of vertices incident to darts and carries the target boundary and both indexed face-boundary forms to that graph via `finiteBalloonBoundaryLoopAt` and `finiteBalloonRelatorLoopAt`; it also proves every supported vertex is reachable from the boundary basepoint. `RelatorBoundaryLoop` packages the relator subloop with defining-relator membership and cyclic reduction, while `RelatorBalloonBoundaryLoop` retains the full attaching word. Each indexed face side now maps to a specific oriented dart with its label and endpoints proved, and the total balloon perimeter equals the literal boundary length, namely the reduced boundary length plus twice the cancellation count. This is still only a finite connected one-skeleton with side-to-dart maps: cyclic orders, planar vertex links, and Euler characteristic are not yet certified.
- The proof deriving Greendlinger's property from reduced van Kampen diagrams and (C'(1/6)) is not yet formalized. This repository is therefore a research prototype, not a Palomar-ready submission.

The remaining bridge must extend the indexed side-to-dart map to a valid planar cell map with cyclic orders and vertex links, then derive its Euler and aggregate incidence accounting, external-face bounds, reducedness-to-piece facts, and shell profile. It must handle cut vertices and semi-exterior vertices, or prove a valid reduction to the disk case. The successive folds realize the free-cancellation trace on a word-occurrence boundary graph. `WalkFoldResult.foldPairsThenReduce` first folds the seed's generated stem-edge pairs and then applies the cancellation trace through the transported quotient maps. Every balloon relator has a closed labeled walk in the final 1-skeleton. The folds still have no proved planar rotation system, and the composed theorem still consumes the complete curvature-and-shell profile as explicit data. The project is not yet Palomar-ready; no intake or registration has been made.
