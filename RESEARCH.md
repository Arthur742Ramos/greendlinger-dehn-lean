# Research case and theorem boundary

## Mathematical target

Let `X` be a finite alphabet and `R` a finite symmetrized set of nonempty cyclically reduced relators satisfying `C'(1/6)`. Greendlinger's lemma says that every nontrivial freely reduced word representing the identity has a cyclic rotation containing a contiguous subword `v` that is an initial segment of some `r ∈ R` and satisfies `2 * |v| > |r|`. This cyclic form is the theorem surface for the executable cyclic Dehn reducer.

The standard proof uses a reduced van Kampen diagram. The `C'(1/6)` condition bounds every internal arc by one sixth of each incident relator. An angle assignment gives interior corners weight at most `2/3` and exterior corners weight at most `1/2`, in units of `π`. Euler characteristic one for the disk makes the total face curvature positive; therefore an exterior shell has at most three internal arcs, and its complementary boundary arc is longer than half its relator.

The finite piece predicate keeps distinct cyclic start positions even when a proper power makes their rotated words equal; the executable checker therefore rejects the periodic relator `a^6` as `C'(1/6)`. Presented-group nullity is also characterized by finite relator-certificate trees. Each certificate flattens to a product of conjugates of defining relators (when the relator list is inverse-closed), and a minimum area exists. `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed` retains such a minimum-area certificate with its ordered relator-conjugate factor occurrences; Lean proves that the occurrence count equals the certificate area. Each occurrence has explicit conjugator and relator labels. Their literal `g r g⁻¹` boundary words freely reduce, factor by factor and then globally, to the requested reduced boundary word. The nested trees replay as adjacent inverse-letter deletions that preserve the free-group element, and each tree's pair count exactly accounts for its own length difference. `SmallCancellation/PlanarCancellation.lean` converts these trees into absolute source positions for cancelled pairs and retained letters. Lean proves that each pair joins inverse source letters, the pairing count is exact, every interval is forward and bounded, and any two intervals are disjoint or nested. Cancellation endpoints and retained source positions are individually distinct, disjoint, and together partition the entire source word; no retained occurrence lies inside a cancellation interval. Retained positions increase strictly in the reduced-word order, and the retained occurrences spell the output while carrying the same labels at their source positions. `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed.boundaryTrace` packages these properties for a direct reduction of the literal conjugate-relator boundary. The new `composeIndexedBoundaryTrace` also composes the factorwise balloon reductions with the global factor cancellation, retaining the exact source positions of both stages in a single `IndexedBoundaryTrace`; `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed.stagedBoundaryTrace` exposes that result. A separate minimum-area lemma proves that no two adjacent factor occurrences are inverses, by replacing them with a certificate two relator leaves smaller. This is algebraic reducedness only; it does not yet establish dipole removal or reducedness for a planar diagram. These results still do not identify edge and vertex quotients or construct a planar disk map. The cyclic executable procedure enumerates every rotation and relator cut, selects a valid long side, replaces that subword by the inverse complementary side, and freely reduces. Each step preserves identity under conjugacy and strictly decreases length. Its termination and word-problem correctness are proved assuming the cyclic Greendlinger property.

`SmallCancellation/Curvature.lean` formalizes the finite Gauss--Bonnet ledger and angle count. Each face stores a finite corner list with actual rational angle values; each corner carries its local 1/2 or 2/3 cap, and the face-level cap is proved by summing those bounds. Side counts and exterior-corner counts are computed from that same list. Its accounting theorem derives total face curvature 2 from Euler's equation and aggregate angle/side incidence counts. `SmallCancellation/Shell.lean` derives the seven-arc interior bound from C'(1/6) piece data, forces a small shell when each internal face has that data, and converts a shell boundary profile into the reducer's cyclic redex. These results compose into a theorem from a finite curvature-and-shell profile to a cyclic redex. `CurvatureShellProfileProperty` packages the exact profile-existence interface; an end-to-end cyclic Dehn correctness theorem is proved assuming that property and `CPrimeSix`. The release-blocking work remains converting the nullity certificate and nested cancellation data into an actual reduced combinatorial diagram, then deriving its Euler/angle identities, external-face bounds, reducedness facts, and shell boundary data. The construction must handle cut vertices and semi-exterior vertices, or prove a valid reduction to the disk case.

`SmallCancellation/DiskMap.lean` supplies a lower incidence layer. A corner is indexed by a vertex and one of its local positions; its angle is assigned as `1 / degree` at a boundary vertex and `2 / degree` at an interior vertex. The local degree lower bounds prove the 1/2 and 2/3 angle caps. Finite-sum regrouping derives the vertex angle total, and per-edge incidence counts derive the total number of face sides plus boundary edges. Together with the disk Euler equation and equal boundary vertex/edge counts, `FaceClassification.to_diskCurvatureAccounting` builds the aggregate ledger consumed by the curvature proof. The composed redex theorem can now consume this incidence data instead of a free-standing `DiskCurvatureAccounting` record. This is not yet a rotation-system or planar-map construction: degree bounds, edge-side counts, boundary-count equality, Euler characteristic, and local face classification remain explicit premises. No theorem yet constructs them from a minimum-area relator certificate.

The same file also proves the numerical shell estimate in isolation: if a relator boundary is partitioned into an exterior arc and at most three internal arcs, and each internal arc is strictly shorter than one sixth of the perimeter, then the exterior arc is longer than half the perimeter. This closes the arithmetic step after an actual diagram supplies those arcs; it does not construct the diagram or establish that its internal arcs are pieces.

`SmallCancellation/Shell.lean` connects that estimate to the executable algorithm: from a specified shell decomposition, piece evidence, same-perimeter relator-prefix evidence, and a cyclic split of the boundary word, it constructs an `IsCyclicRedex` witness. The stronger symmetrized-presentation theorem derives those prefix bounds from the shell relator's cyclic positions.

`SmallCancellation/Words.lean` now proves that the presentation's one-step rotation closure extends to every cyclic cut while preserving the exact rotated word; cyclic reduction rules out cancellation at each new join. The stronger shell theorem uses this result to derive the same-perimeter prefix bounds from the actual cyclic positions of the pieces on the shell relator.

The same module proves that a complete relator boundary partitioned into C'(1/6) pieces must contain at least seven arcs. With the exact side count from its corner list and the derived local angle cap, this derives nonpositive curvature for an internal face. Per-vertex angle closure, edge pairing, and disk Euler characteristic still need to be derived from an actual combinatorial diagram. It also composes these face bounds with `DiskCurvatureAccounting` to force a small shell when every internal face has explicit piece data. `ShellRedexData` then turns the selected shell into the exact cyclic redex. A real diagram must still supply those partitions, Euler and aggregate incidence identities, external/shell face classifications, and shell boundary profiles.

## Why this theorem matters

Greendlinger's result is the classical bridge from a local overlap restriction on relators to an effective decision procedure for the word problem. The target combines substantial combinatorial group theory with a verified executable algorithm. The repository builds on Mathlib's free-group and presented-group foundations and adds the finite small-cancellation definitions, executable relator checker, exhaustive Dehn reducer, and conditional correctness theorems.

Primary references:

- Martin Greendlinger, “Dehn's algorithm for the word problem,” *Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 13 (1960), 67–83. [DOI and publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160130108).
- Roger C. Lyndon, “On Dehn's Algorithm,” *Mathematische Annalen* 166 (1966), 208–228. [EuDML record](https://eudml.org/doc/161458).

An accessible exposition of the arc-reduced diagram and angle-counting proof is Martin T. Touikan, *An Introduction to Combinatorial Group Theory*, §3.5. [Course notes](https://ntouikan.ext.unb.ca/MATH6022/IntroCGGT/html_output/section-18.html).
