# Research case and theorem boundary

## Mathematical target

Let `X` be a finite alphabet and `R` a finite symmetrized set of nonempty cyclically reduced relators satisfying `C'(1/6)`. Greendlinger's lemma says that every nontrivial freely reduced word representing the identity has a cyclic rotation containing a contiguous subword `v` that is an initial segment of some `r ∈ R` and satisfies `2 * |v| > |r|`. This cyclic form is the theorem surface for the executable cyclic Dehn reducer.

The standard proof uses a reduced van Kampen diagram. The `C'(1/6)` condition bounds every internal arc by one sixth of each incident relator. An angle assignment gives interior corners weight at most `2/3` and exterior corners weight at most `1/2`, in units of `π`. Euler characteristic one for the disk makes the total face curvature positive; therefore an exterior shell has at most three internal arcs, and its complementary boundary arc is longer than half its relator.

The finite piece predicate keeps distinct cyclic start positions even when a proper power makes their rotated words equal; the executable checker therefore rejects the periodic relator `a^6` as `C'(1/6)`. Presented-group nullity is also characterized by finite relator-certificate trees. Each certificate flattens to a product of conjugates of defining relators (when the relator list is inverse-closed), and a minimum area exists. `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed` retains such a minimum-area certificate with its ordered relator-conjugate factor occurrences; Lean proves that the occurrence count equals the certificate area. Each occurrence has explicit conjugator and relator labels. Their literal `g r g⁻¹` boundary words freely reduce, factor by factor and then globally, to the requested reduced boundary word. The nested trees replay as adjacent inverse-letter deletions that preserve the free-group element, and each tree's pair count exactly accounts for its own length difference. `SmallCancellation/PlanarCancellation.lean` converts these trees into absolute source positions for cancelled pairs and retained letters. Lean proves that each pair joins inverse source letters, the pairing count is exact, every interval is forward and bounded, and any two intervals are disjoint or nested. Cancellation endpoints and retained source positions are individually distinct, disjoint, and together partition the entire source word; no retained occurrence lies inside a cancellation interval. Retained positions increase strictly in the reduced-word order, and the retained occurrences spell the output while carrying the same labels at their source positions. For a composed trace, every position inside a cancellation interval is another cancellation endpoint paired strictly inside that interval, and the first interior position opens a nested pair. `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed.boundaryTrace` packages these properties for a direct reduction of the literal conjugate-relator boundary. The new `composeIndexedBoundaryTrace` also composes the factorwise balloon reductions with the global factor cancellation, retaining the exact source positions of both stages in a single `IndexedBoundaryTrace`; `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed.stagedBoundaryTrace` exposes that result. Minimum area rules out adjacent inverse factors and inverse first/last factors; the latter contradiction conjugates the middle factor list and saves two relator leaves. It also rules out any nonempty consecutive sublist with product one, and any wrapping cyclic interval with product one after conjugating the complementary factor list, since either deletion lowers area. This is algebraic reducedness only; it does not yet establish dipole removal or reducedness for a planar diagram. These algebraic results do not yet construct a planar disk map; the successive-fold construction below now realizes their cancellation traces as labeled edge and vertex quotients. The fold map from an arbitrary finite list of inverse-labeled dart pairs has now been proved exact: equality in the final quotient is equivalent to the equivalence relation generated by those pair folds. For the seed's stem and cancellation pairings, connected components correspond exactly to unoriented dart classes, and the global flattened relator-side occurrence list is injective. Thus each final unoriented edge class contains at most two relator-side occurrences in total, not merely at most two occurrences from any one balloon. The global cancellation pairs cannot simply be united with the lollipop stem pairings: a source occurrence in a doubled stem edge can also be consumed by a cancellation at the join of two factors. The quotient construction must fold edges successively and transport the edge involution and vertex classes at each step. The cyclic executable procedure enumerates every rotation and relator cut, selects a valid long side, replaces that subword by the inverse complementary side, and freely reduces. Each step preserves identity under conjugacy and strictly decreases length. Its termination and word-problem correctness are proved assuming the cyclic Greendlinger property.

`SmallCancellation/ReducedLollipop.lean` closes the factorwise-reduction step for finite symmetrized presentations. Given any factor conjugate to a relator, select a conjugacy witness with minimum conjugator length. If the literal `g r g⁻¹` word cancelled where the conjugator met the relator on either side, rotating the relator at that letter would produce a valid witness with a shorter conjugator. Thus both junctions are reduced; the raw lollipop boundary is reduced and equals the factor's canonical free-group word. The selected witness retains its minimum-length property. Each relator-factor occurrence can consequently be represented by a reduced relator balloon without changing its word or relator-cell label. Lean proves that each balloon boundary has length `2 * |g| + |r|`; for the normalized seed, the total boundary length is the reduced boundary length plus twice the global cancellation count. The concatenation of normalized balloon boundaries is exactly the raw factor word, and `reducedLollipopBoundaryTrace` records the remaining global noncrossing cancellations. This remains source-word data: it does not construct the quotient complex, prove that it is planar or disk-like, or derive edge and vertex incidence counts.

For a minimum-area certificate of area one, `MinimalAreaRelatorBoundarySeed.cyclicGreendlinger_of_area_one` proves the cyclic Dehn-redex conclusion directly. The unique factor is the target word, and its reduced lollipop boundary contains the entire defining relator, whose complementary arc is empty. This isolates the base case; it does not address interactions among multiple relator faces.

`cyclicGreendlinger_of_certificate_area_le_one` lifts that base case back to presented-group nullity: if any relator certificate has area at most one, then every nontrivial null word has a cyclic redex. It obtains a minimum-area seed from nullity, uses minimality to bound that seed's area by the supplied certificate, and rules out area zero because the word is nontrivial. The corollary `cyclicDehnReduce_norm_lt_of_certificate_area_le_one` proves the executable reducer strictly shortens every such input. More generally, `cyclicDehnReduceFuel_norm_le` shows fuel-bounded reduction cannot increase norm when fuel covers the input norm, and `cyclicDehnReduce_norm_lt_of_cyclicRedex` proves strict progress from any available redex.

`SmallCancellation/Curvature.lean` formalizes the finite Gauss--Bonnet ledger and angle count. Each face stores a finite corner list with actual rational angle values; each corner carries its local 1/2 or 2/3 cap, and the face-level cap is proved by summing those bounds. Side counts and exterior-corner counts are computed from that same list. Its accounting theorem derives total face curvature 2 from Euler's equation and aggregate angle/side incidence counts. `SmallCancellation/Shell.lean` derives the seven-arc interior bound from C'(1/6) piece data, forces a small shell when each internal face has that data, and converts a shell boundary profile into the reducer's cyclic redex. These results compose into a theorem from a finite curvature-and-shell profile to a cyclic redex. `CurvatureShellProfileProperty` now packages profile existence only for cyclically reduced boundary words. A proved transfer lemma uses Mathlib's cyclic reduction and conjugator to extend a Greendlinger shell from this core to a cyclic rotation of the original word, so the executable Dehn theorem retains its all-word statement. End-to-end cyclic Dehn correctness is proved assuming this property and `CPrimeSix`. The release-blocking work remains converting the nullity certificate and nested cancellation data into an actual reduced combinatorial diagram, then deriving its Euler/angle identities, external-face bounds, reducedness facts, and shell boundary data. The construction must handle cut vertices and semi-exterior vertices, or prove a valid reduction to the disk case.

`SmallCancellation/DiskMap.lean` supplies a lower incidence layer. A corner is indexed by a vertex and one of its local positions; its angle is assigned as `1 / degree` at a boundary vertex and `2 / degree` at an interior vertex. The local degree lower bounds prove the 1/2 and 2/3 angle caps. Finite-sum regrouping derives the vertex angle total, and per-edge incidence counts derive the total number of face sides plus boundary edges. Together with the disk Euler equation and equal boundary vertex/edge counts, `FaceClassification.to_diskCurvatureAccounting` builds the aggregate ledger consumed by the curvature proof. The composed redex theorem can now consume this incidence data instead of a free-standing `DiskCurvatureAccounting` record. This is not yet a rotation-system or planar-map construction: degree bounds, edge-side counts, boundary-count equality, Euler characteristic, and local face classification remain explicit premises. No theorem yet constructs them from a minimum-area relator certificate.

`SmallCancellation/VertexLinks.lean` gives the corresponding generalized local ledger for non-manifold points created by folding boundary edges. A circle link contributes angle sum 2 and Euler defect 0; an interval contributes angle sum 1 and defect 1; a disconnected link is assigned angle sum 0 and must have defect at least 2. The module proves that every such vertex contributes at least 2 after adding its link Euler characteristic, and it derives `LinkCurvatureAccounting` from finite corner/face incidence data and the global edge-dart and Euler equations. `CurvatureShellProfile` now consumes this generalized link record. This closes the abstract curvature accounting for cut and semi-exterior vertices, but not their construction: no quotient of the boundary cancellation trace has yet been shown to have these links, incidence counts, Euler characteristic, or a planar embedding.

`SmallCancellation/EdgeFold.lean` makes one step of the missing quotient operation precise. Given an involution on a dart type and two boundary darts to be glued in opposite orientations, it generates the two corresponding dart identifications and proves the involution descends to the quotient. Thus successive folds can be composed without losing the edge-reversal operation. The module does not yet prove that the quotient involution is fixed-point-free, update endpoint/rotation data, preserve planar embedding, or produce the link and Euler records consumed by the curvature proof.

`SmallCancellation/DartGraphFold.lean` adds the 1-skeleton endpoints and signed labels to that fold. Source and target maps descend through the vertex quotient, the induced reversal still exchanges endpoints, and an inverse-labeled pair of boundary darts yields a label on the folded dart quotient. A `DartWalk` maps into the folded graph, and the endpoint equality from a fold deletes a consecutive backtrack while preserving a valid residual walk. Its fold-list theorem proves the dart map's kernel is exactly the generated pair-fold relation. This still does not define a rotation system or facial walks, prove the folded map is planar, or derive C'(1/6) piece arcs and curvature incidence.

The word-indexed extension in `SmallCancellation/DartGraphFold.lean` records a walk together with the exact word read on its darts. It proves decomposition at word concatenations, extracts the dart in a one-letter walk, realizes each adjacent free cancellation as a fold, and composes the graph maps across a full cancellation sequence. Surjectivity of each quotient map proves that the final dart type remains finite. Applying this to a `FreeReductionShape` produces successive quotients and a residual walk with the reduced label. The pair-fold result additionally tracks every supplied inverse-labeled pair through later folds and proves its two images are opposite orientations of one quotient edge; the exact-kernel theorem shows these are all of the dart identifications. `SmallCancellation/WordPath.lean` supplies an occurrence-indexed path, prefix/suffix embeddings, a polygonal boundary circuit, and a prefix walk to the source of each edge occurrence. `SmallCancellation/LollipopFolds.lean` generates the stem-pair list and endpoint joins for all normalized balloons in the flattened seed boundary. It proves that every balloon relator segment, including one with empty conjugator, gives a closed relator-labeled walk in the final folded 1-skeleton, after global boundary cancellations are also applied. The choice definition `balloonRelatorLoopAt` packages the relator subloop for each balloon occurrence. A new theorem also carries the complete conjugate-relator attaching word as a closed walk after the stem and global boundary folds. `SmallCancellation/FiniteSupport.lean` restricts the quotient graph to the finite set of vertices incident to darts; every vertex of that support is incident to a dart. `finiteBalloonRelatorLoopAt` and `finiteBalloonBoundaryLoopAt` transport the target boundary and both indexed face-boundary forms into that common finite graph, and `finiteFoldedBoundaryVertex_reachable` proves every supported vertex is joined to the boundary basepoint by a labeled walk. `RelatorBoundaryLoop` retains defining-relator membership and cyclic reduction for the relator subloop, while `RelatorBalloonBoundaryLoop` retains the full attaching word. `SmallCancellation/FiniteDartEdges.lean` proves that reversal classes each contain exactly two darts, that every supported vertex has positive out-degree, and that summing out-degrees over the finite support counts twice the number of unoriented edges. A new occurrence-indexed side map sends each balloon side to a specific oriented dart and proves its label, source, and target; the full loop is built from that same indexed path map. The aggregate balloon perimeter is exactly the literal boundary length, equal to the reduced boundary length plus twice the global cancellation count. This gives side-to-dart incidence data, but no certified cyclic orders, vertex links, or Euler characteristic.

The same file also proves the numerical shell estimate in isolation: if a relator boundary is partitioned into an exterior arc and at most three internal arcs, and each internal arc is strictly shorter than one sixth of the perimeter, then the exterior arc is longer than half the perimeter. This closes the arithmetic step after an actual diagram supplies those arcs; it does not construct the diagram or establish that its internal arcs are pieces.

`SmallCancellation/Shell.lean` connects that estimate to the executable algorithm: from a specified shell decomposition, piece evidence, same-perimeter relator-prefix evidence, and a cyclic split of the boundary word, it constructs an `IsCyclicRedex` witness. The stronger symmetrized-presentation theorem derives those prefix bounds from the shell relator's cyclic positions.

`SmallCancellation/Words.lean` now proves that the presentation's one-step rotation closure extends to every cyclic cut while preserving the exact rotated word; cyclic reduction rules out cancellation at each new join. The stronger shell theorem uses this result to derive the same-perimeter prefix bounds from the actual cyclic positions of the pieces on the shell relator.

The same module proves that a complete relator boundary partitioned into C'(1/6) pieces must contain at least seven arcs. With the exact side count from its corner list and the derived local angle cap, this derives nonpositive curvature for an internal face. Per-vertex angle closure, edge pairing, and disk Euler characteristic still need to be derived from an actual combinatorial diagram. It also composes these face bounds with `DiskCurvatureAccounting` to force a small shell when every internal face has explicit piece data. `ShellRedexData` then turns the selected shell into the exact cyclic redex. A real diagram must still supply those partitions, Euler and aggregate incidence identities, external/shell face classifications, and shell boundary profiles.

## Why this theorem matters

Greendlinger's result is the classical bridge from a local overlap restriction on relators to an effective decision procedure for the word problem. The target combines substantial combinatorial group theory with a verified executable algorithm. The repository builds on Mathlib's free-group and presented-group foundations and adds the finite small-cancellation definitions, executable relator checker, exhaustive Dehn reducer, and conditional correctness theorems.

Primary references:

- Martin Greendlinger, “Dehn's algorithm for the word problem,” *Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 13 (1960), 67–83. [DOI and publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160130108).
- Roger C. Lyndon, “On Dehn's Algorithm,” *Mathematische Annalen* 166 (1966), 208–228. [EuDML record](https://eudml.org/doc/161458).

An accessible exposition of the arc-reduced diagram and angle-counting proof is Martin T. Touikan, *An Introduction to Combinatorial Group Theory*, §3.5. [Course notes](https://ntouikan.ext.unb.ca/MATH6022/IntroCGGT/html_output/section-18.html).

The current endpoint development counts each relator-side occurrence and surviving target-boundary occurrence as a separate incidence on an edge class of the explicit pair-fold quotient. Every edge class represented by either kind has exactly two incidences. The three cases are two relator sides, one relator side plus one surviving boundary occurrence, or two surviving boundary occurrences. `SmallCancellation/PairFoldIncidenceLedger.lean` packages these fibers and proves that the total relator perimeter plus reduced boundary length equals twice the number of used edge classes. `SmallCancellation/FoldedTraceReduction.lean` and `StemPairEndpoints.lean` now prove that all recorded cancellation pairs are already opposite dart occurrences in this quotient, so the reduced target boundary is obtained by deleting backtracks while holding the graph fixed. The reduced-walk record's graph is proved equal to the incidence-ledger graph. This supplies the edge-side count and an actual reduced boundary walk on that graph, including stem-only edges, but it does not yet establish planarity or supply the vertex links and Euler characteristic needed by the curvature proof. Equivalence to the separate sequential fold-then-reduce quotient remains unproved.

`SmallCancellation/PairFoldIncidenceLedger.lean` now also preserves the exact relator letter on every face-side dart and proves that the relator letters at the two face-side incidences of a shared folded edge are inverse. This establishes the one-edge label compatibility needed to build longer common arcs. Those arcs are not yet classified as pieces: that requires a dipole-free diagram argument, identification of maximal connected overlaps, and control of repeated cyclic positions.

For one edge, `pairFoldFaceSide_singletonPiece_of_noDipole` now converts two specified cyclic cuts of the incident relators into a one-letter `IsPiece`, provided the resulting rotated relators are distinct. The cuts and distinctness condition expose the reduced-diagram obligation directly. Minimum-area algebraic factor data have not yet been shown to imply it.

`SmallCancellation/Words.lean` now proves the base case for that classification: a shared one-letter prefix is a piece whenever it comes from distinct symmetrized relators, or from two distinct cyclic positions of the same relator. The second case retains the position tags needed for periodic relators. The diagram-derived distinctness premise remains open.

`SmallCancellation/PairFoldRelatorLoops.lean` now carries every balloon's defining-relator loop into the direct pair-fold quotient, using the innermost stem pair or the empty-stem endpoint join. The quotient has both the reduced exterior boundary walk and all indexed relator loops, but it is not yet proved planar and has no constructed vertex-link or Euler data for the curvature argument.

`SmallCancellation/PairFoldFiniteComplex.lean` restricts this direct quotient to the finite set of vertices incident to darts and proves every supported vertex reachable from the boundary basepoint. The target boundary and every indexed relator loop now live on one finite connected graph; incidence labels and opposite orientations survive the support restriction, while the pair-fold ledger gives exact two-element fibers over used edges. These are finite polygonal attachment data, not yet a certified planar map: cyclic orders, vertex-link shapes, and the Euler characteristic still have to be derived from the cancellation pairings.

The direct finite graph now also has an occurrence-indexed graph map from each relator polygon. Its forward dart at cyclic position `j` is proved equal to the reverse of the ledger incidence dart indexed by the same balloon occurrence and position. The endpoint theorem closes that same mapped path at its occurrence-specific base vertex, using the empty-stem endpoint join or the indexed innermost stem fold. The source and target of each positive side are also identified with the target and source of that reversed face-side incidence dart. `pairFoldFiniteRelatorCellAt` packages the result as a defining-relator loop in the finite graph. This supplies a side-and-endpoint attachment-to-ledger correspondence and closed face loops, but no vertex-link system, rotation system, or Euler proof.

The indexed finite complex now has explicit face-endpoint germs. A cyclic predecessor/successor involution pairs the two germs at each corner of a relator polygon; Lean proves this pairing has no fixed germs and that its endpoints map to the same vertex, including the cyclic basepoint. For two distinct face sides in one folded-edge class, the exact two-incidence ledger proves there is at most one partner, and opposite dart orientations identify source-to-target and target-to-source endpoint germs across the edge. The resulting edge-step relation is symmetric, stays at one quotient vertex, and has at most one partner per germ. `SmallCancellation/PairFoldVertexLink.lean` packages corner and edge steps as a finite transition graph on these face-endpoint germs and proves its degree is at most two. This graph records local connectivity, but it is not itself the topological link whose vertices are edge ends and whose edges are corners.

`SmallCancellation/PairFoldCellLink.lean` now defines that local corner-incidence multigraph at each quotient vertex. Its vertices are all outgoing oriented edge ends at the vertex, including stem-only ends; each relator-corner occurrence contributes a distinct edge, so parallel corners remain represented. The endpoint-germ map proves that a face-shared edge identifies the corresponding oriented ends. It also proves every corner edge is loopless: an endpoint collapse would force adjacent letters in a relator to be inverse, contradicted by free reducedness, or by cyclic reducedness at the polygon basepoint. Each endpoint-incidence fiber has cardinality at most two, by bijecting it with face germs and injecting those into the exact two-incidence edge ledger. Thus every link vertex has corner degree at most two. Summing the endpoint degrees proves that at each quotient vertex the number of corner occurrences is at most the number of oriented edge ends. This is a local Euler-defect bound; it does not yet classify connected components as paths or cycles, establish compatible rotation data, or derive the global Euler characteristic from the minimum-area certificate.

Repository and code searches have not found a Lean formalization of this theorem. This is a search result, not a claim of global priority. Palomar eligibility will be evaluated only after the exact theorem is fully proved and all required release gates pass.
